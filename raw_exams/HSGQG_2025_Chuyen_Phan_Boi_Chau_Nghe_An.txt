SECTION III: READING
READING PASSAGE:
Throughout the nineteenth century, classical economists conceptualized human decision-making through the prism of Homo economicus—an idealized agent endowed with unbounded computational capacity and stable self-interest. However, the pioneering work of Daniel Kahneman and Amos Tversky dismantled this axiomatic edifice by demonstrating that human judgment under uncertainty is systematically skewed by cognitive heuristics. Rather than calculating actuarial probabilities from scratch, individuals rely on mental shortcuts such as the availability heuristic, whereby the perceived likelihood of an event is dictated by how Vividly examples come to mind.

Furthermore, Prospect Theory revealed that human sensitivity to losses is markedly asymmetrical relative to equivalent gains: the psychological pain of losing one hundred dollars is roughly twice as intense as the gratification of winning the same sum.
