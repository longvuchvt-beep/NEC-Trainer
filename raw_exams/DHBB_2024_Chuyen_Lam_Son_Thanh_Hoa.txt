Intriguingly, isotopic carbon tracing demonstrates that mature canopy "hub trees" transfer surplus carbon and defense signaling molecules to shaded understory seedlings during insect infestations.

READING PASSAGE:
The phenomenon of urban shrinkage—defined as sustained population loss accompanied by structural economic transformation in post-industrial cities—poses a formidable challenge to conventional municipal planning. Because twentieth-century urban governance was predicated almost exclusively on managing demographic expansion, metropolitan infrastructure such as water mains, transit grids, and school districts suffers from severe diseconomies of scale when population density contracts by thirty percent or more. Rather than pursuing futile growth-chasing subsidies, progressive urbanists advocate "smart decline": decommissioning redundant grey infrastructure and converting vacant industrial parcels into urban carbon sinks and community wetlands.
